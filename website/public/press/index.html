Who we are, in one line

Lightbound builds Gauss, which shows satellite teams which of their data and onboard-AI results they can trust after the space environment turns rough, and runs Constellation, a free, invite-only space community.

Who we are, in a paragraph

Lightbound Limited is a UK company (England and Wales, company no. 14573199) with its founder based in Malaysia. Its product, Gauss, replays a satellite team's telemetry and onboard-AI logs against the space-weather record and reports, record by record, what held up, what didn't and what is unknown. Gauss is advisory: it never commands a spacecraft, and it doesn't claim flight readiness. Lightbound also runs Constellation, a community for people working in and around space, with events, workshops and Gauss for Education for universities.

Where our names come from

Gauss. In 1801 the first asteroid ever found, Ceres, slipped behind the Sun after six weeks of watching. Carl Friedrich Gauss, aged 24, worked out its orbit from that short arc, and in December 1801 it was found again close to where he said. A year later Heinrich Olbers found Pallas; Gauss’s 1810 paper on its orbit is where the method engineers call Gaussian elimination takes its name. In 1832 Gauss made the first absolute measurement of Earth’s magnetic field, the field Gauss the product watches as solar storms push on it.

Lightbound. Named for a man who gave hope and joy through the Second World War. The rest is the founder’s to tell, in person.

Stories we can help with

  • When the Sun scrambles an AI in orbit A growing number of satellites run AI models on ordinary computer chips. Radiation can flip bits in those chips, and a corrupted answer looks exactly like a good one. We can explain how, and what teams can do about it on the ground.
  • The storm of January 2026 On 19 January 2026 NOAA declared a severe (S4) solar radiation storm, the first since 2003, and warned of memory upsets on satellites. We can explain what that means for AI in orbit.
  • Solar storms, live During a storm we can comment on what NOAA's radiation (S), geomagnetic (G) and radio-blackout (R) levels mean for satellites, in plain words, from the same public data our site shows.
  • One storm, replayed Our replay walks a storm record by record. It is synthetic and labelled as such, so it is safe to show on screen or in a video.
  • Space communities in South-East Asia Constellation brings together students, engineers and founders, with workshops on ethics, resilience and tech leadership.

What we won't say

We won't say Gauss has passed any standard or approval, or that it is ready to fly; we won't give radiation dose or hardware damage figures, or name any organisation we work with without its written agreement. If a draft says otherwise, we'll ask you to correct it.
