01_GAUSS · FLEET INTEGRATION

Know which satellite data and onboard-AI outputs you can trust.

Radiation and space weather can silently corrupt telemetry and AI results in orbit. A corrupted output looks the same as a good one. Gauss replays your telemetry and inference logs against the space environment they were produced in, and shows your ground team what held up, what didn't, and what is unknown.

Built for mission-data, ground-segment and mission-operations teams, and onboard-AI and orbital-compute teams who need evidence, not dashboards. Fleet Integration is a ground-first mission-data assurance and protection layer.

02_HOW_IT_WORKS

How a replay pilot works

Fleet Integration v0.1 runs on the ground, on your own logs. Nothing is installed on a spacecraft.

  1. Bring your logs

    Telemetry (CCSDS/XTCE-shaped packets or Yamcs exports) and onboard inference logs, shared under a data-sharing agreement.

  2. Correlate with the space environment

    Each record is lined up with the space-weather conditions at the time: radiation and geomagnetic activity from public NOAA and NASA HAPI services.

  3. Score quality and provenance

    Every value carries its source and age. Bounded inference with a deterministic fallback flags outputs taken under stress. Missing or stale inputs stay unknown, never "nominal".

  4. Get the evidence

    A report your engineers and assurance reviewers can check, with success metrics agreed before the pilot starts.

Working today: live NOAA and NASA HAPI space-weather ingest; three L1 spacecraft cross-checked, with a flag when SOLAR-1 and IMAP disagree on density; the solar wind propagated to Earth for about 50 minutes of warning; a Kp 3-hour forecast that beats persistence on held-out 2022–24 data and says plainly that it is worse during storms; radiation (S), geomagnetic (G) and radio-blackout (R) levels kept apart, computed from GOES protons, Kp and GOES X-rays to NOAA’s thresholds and cross-checked against NOAA’s published scales; every input sense-checked, so inconsistent sensor data is flagged with a reason instead of dropped; stale or missing inputs shown as unknown (S?, G?, R?), never as quiet. Being built for the first pilots: ingest of your own logs, output flagging and the evidence export.

03_CLAIMS

What we claim, and what we don't

We will show you

  • Which records were produced under elevated space-weather conditions (proton, electron and geomagnetic levels)
  • Where data quality or provenance is missing, with "unknown" said plainly
  • Simulation results labelled as simulation

We don't claim

  • Flight readiness or space qualification
  • Radiation dose or hardware damage figures
  • Any ability to command a spacecraft: Gauss is advisory only

05_FIT

Where Gauss fits

We integrate at standard boundaries. We don't replace your mission control.

  • CCSDS and XTCE Packet identity and telemetry definitions, as your ground segment already uses them.
  • Yamcs (pilot integration) Designed to replay from mission-control archives; tested on sample data so far.

06_CONNECTIVITY

Assured Connectivity for the team on the ground.

Argo keeps an operations team's laptops and ground hosts working on hotspots, hotel and café Wi-Fi. It finds what broke, fixes what it safely can, and tells a person what it can't.

  • Self-healing Checks the connection every minute, fixes DNS and proxy faults, opens Wi-Fi login pages, and remembers what worked on each network. One alert per problem, not one every few minutes.
  • Power, memory and disk Sheds optional work when memory, battery or disk run short, and keeps a closed laptop working down to 10% battery.
  • Wi-Fi security Flags weakly encrypted networks and devices left open to everyone on them. Advice only: it never changes your settings.
  • Approved exit countries Keeps traffic inside an approved-country policy and says when a SIM or network breaks it.
  • Team status Each device reports to the Gauss operator console, and a phone that drops off the team network gets a text with the fix.
  • Mac and Linux Runs on macOS laptops, and on Linux ground hosts as a small, locked-down container.

Running today on Lightbound's own laptops. Offered to pilot teams, scoped per team.
